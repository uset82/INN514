# Theme 6: Technological innovation systems and their functions

## Core definitions
- **Technological innovation system** (*teknologisk innovasjonssystem*): citing Carlsson and Stankiewicz, a network of agents that create, spread and use a technology under shared institutions. (Hekkert et al. 2007, p. 416)
- **Function** (*funksjon*): an activity that results in technological change; Hekkert and colleagues list seven. (Hekkert et al. 2007, p. 415)
- **Guidance of the search** (*retning for søket*): the function that sets expectations and goals and so steers where a technology goes. (Hekkert et al. 2007, p. 423)
- **Materialization** (*materialisering*): the function that covers large physical infrastructure, added because CCS needs it. (Steen et al. 2024, p. 2)
- **Legitimation** (*legitimering*): the function that covers acceptance and legitimacy. (Steen et al. 2024, p. 3)

## Key models and figures
**Seven functions of innovation systems** (Hekkert et al. 2007, pp. 421–425):
1. Entrepreneurial activity
2. Knowledge development
3. Knowledge diffusion through networks
4. Guidance of the search
5. Market formation
6. Resource mobilization
7. Creation of legitimacy

**Three motors of change** (Hekkert et al. 2007, p. 426):
1. Motor A: lobbying for market formation, then entrepreneurial activity.
2. Motor B: lobbying for research resources, then higher expectations.
3. Motor C: guidance of the search, then resources and knowledge.

## Norway examples
- **Longship and Northern Lights**: the main exceptions to weak materialization: large projects now being built with state support. (Steen et al. 2024, p. 10)
- **Maritime batteries**: a cluster set a direction, public procurement created the market, and some seventy ferry lines followed. (Njøs et al. 2020, pp. 281–282)
- **Mongstad**: an earlier plan for a full-scale plant at the refinery and power station. (Steen et al. 2024, p. 4)

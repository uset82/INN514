## Core definitions
- **Innovation system** (*innovasjonssystem*): learning and innovation across the whole economy, not only in universities and research institutes. (Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 431)
- **National innovation system** (*nasjonalt innovasjonssystem*): the same at the scale of a country, a term the authors trace to Freeman, Lundvall and Nelson. (Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 431)
- **Institutions** (*institusjoner*): citing North, the rules of the game, costly to establish but scale-giving once adopted. (Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 433)
- **Path dependency** (*stiavhengighet*): historical processes that favour some solutions and hold others back. (Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 431)
- **Trinity of innovation** (*treenighet*): citing Schwarz and Thompson, the patterns of people, things and ideas behind technology. (Fløysand et al. 2013, PDF p. 9)

## Key models and figures
**Three industrial development paths** (Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 439):
1. Small-scale decentralized path: small firms investing little of their own funds.
2. Large-scale centralized path: metals, electrochemistry and hydropower, often foreign-financed.
3. Knowledge-intensive network-based path: R&D-intensive firms in sectors such as ICT.

**The pragmatic innovation complex** (Fløysand et al. 2013, PDF p. 10):
1. Company and tools meet through technique.
2. Tools and tasks meet through production, with unintended outputs.
3. Company and tasks meet through rationale, profit.

## Norway examples
- **Domestic petroleum suppliers**: shipbuilding, engineering and services grew with petroleum instead of Dutch disease. (Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 434)
- **The concession laws**: early waterfall rules became the model for national control of offshore oil and gas, decades later. (Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 441)
- **Fish-farming**: disease control was mastered without a large corporate laboratory, building on regional resources. (Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 441)

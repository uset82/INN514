## 1. Read the question
<!-- readings: Class 9, slides 33–43 -->

How can agency in innovation systems promote or inhibit sustainable transformation? Use examples.

The job of this part is to take that question apart before you write. Slides 34 and 35 of Class 9 ask you to show how you read the task, not to rush into a definition.

There are three parts. First, the concept is agency, set inside innovation systems, and the aim is sustainable transformation. Second, the command is promote or inhibit, so both sides are required. Third, use examples, so the case must be Norwegian and you must be able to name its source. Those were the three parts.

Only Question 1 is compulsory. You then choose Question 2 or Question 3. The two answers are weighted equally, and no aids are allowed. Answers can be tested for plagiarism, so learn the moves and use your own words.

A weaker answer would define agency and stop. A stronger answer would keep both sides and tie them to one sourced case.

One way to say it: By this question, I understand a demand for both sides and one sourced Norwegian case.

Remember: both sides, then a Norwegian case you can source.

## 3. The introduction
<!-- readings: Class 9, slides 39, 43; Fløysand et al. 2022, p. 17 -->

The job of this part is the introduction. Slide 39 of Class 9 says to present how you understand the question and how you have chosen to answer it. You may also name one main argument. Slide 43 then checks whether the problem statement follows the question you were given.

In plain words, the opening tells the reader the route. More precisely, you say that you will treat agency as work that can alter a path or keep it going, and that you will test both sides on two petroleum regions.

Sustainable transformation, as the exam-day sheet takes it from Fløysand and colleagues, citing UNEP, is change that cuts emissions and protects biodiversity. You do not need a longer definition in the first lines. You do need the reader to see that the question is about that kind of change, not about novelty for its own sake.

A weaker answer would open with a history of the oil industry. A stronger answer would state the route and the case in a few lines.

One way to say it: To discuss how agency can help or hold back this change, I will define two kinds of work, then use the two regions.

Remember: the introduction states the route and the case.

## 4. The theory you need
<!-- readings: Bækkelund 2021, pp. 758, 759; Garud & Karnøe 2003, p. 277; Schlaile et al. 2017, p. 5; Class 9, slide 40 -->

The job of this part is the theory. Slide 40 of Class 9 says to start from the readings, clarify the concepts, and put those readings to use. Revision six explains the agency range in full. Here you only need the pieces this question uses.

In plain words, agency is someone acting so that a path changes or stays. More precisely, Bækkelund, 2021, citing Sotarauta and Suvinen, treats agency as action that produces an effect, whether intended or not. Change agency alters a path. Reproductive agency keeps a path going. The reading does not call either kind sustainable or unsustainable.

Garud and Karnøe, 2003, add a second point. Agency on a technology path is distributed across many actors. A single heroic inventor is the wrong picture. Their contrast is bricolage, citing Levi-Strauss, modest steps with what is at hand, against a breakthrough attempt.

Schlaile and colleagues, 2017, name the aim. On the exam-day sheet, sustainability includes ecological continuity, justice within and between generations, and quality of life.

A weaker answer would list author names and stop. A stronger answer would use each name for one job in the argument.

One way to say it: By agency, I understand work that can alter a path or keep it going, and that work is shared.

Remember: two kinds of agency, distributed across actors, aimed at a real sustainability question.

## 5. How it can promote
<!-- readings: Njøs et al. 2020, pp. 279, 281, 282; Fløysand et al. 2022, pp. 15, 23; Bækkelund 2021, p. 758; Garud & Karnøe 2003, pp. 277–279 -->

The job of this part is the promote side. The question's command requires it, and slide 40 of Class 9 asks you to relate the readings to the case, not to praise change in the abstract.

There are three ways, and each one stays inside a reading. First, change agency can alter a path. Bækkelund describes that work as entrepreneurship, institutional work or leadership. Second, many actors can improve a technology in modest steps. Garud and Karnøe read Danish wind that way, as bricolage, and they read the United States path as a breakthrough attempt. Third, a region can point an existing industry at a new use. Njøs and colleagues report that a cluster set a direction for maritime batteries and that public procurement created a market.

Fløysand and colleagues add the regional form of the same idea. In diversified Hordaland, collective work legitimised a green shift. An intermediary in that study says forty to fifty oil firms now also supply ocean farming. That is promotion only if you keep the source. It is not a promise that every project is sustainable.

A weaker answer would say actors are important. A stronger answer would name the mechanism and the reading.

Remember: promotion is change work, shared experiment, and a region that legitimises the new path.

## 6. How it can inhibit
<!-- readings: Bækkelund 2021, p. 758; Fløysand et al. 2022, pp. 15, 22, 24, 25; Class 9, slide 31 -->

The job of this part is the inhibit side. If you only write the hopeful half, you have not answered the question. Slide 43 of Class 9 looks for analysis that can be critical, not only for a list of benefits.

In plain words, the same kind of effort can hold a region on the old path. More precisely, reproductive agency keeps a path going. Bækkelund is careful here. Reproductive agency can be resistance to what is new, but it is not only resistance, and it is action in its own right. The model answer goes further and calls that protection of an unsustainable dependence. Prefer Bækkelund's own wording. That wording does not make that sustainability judgement.

Fløysand and colleagues show the regional form. In petroleum-specialised Rogaland, individual-oriented work delegitimised a green shift. Their account links that pattern to individual strategic leadership, and to petroleum activity that continued as oil and gas markets recovered. The lock-in account runs from a recession that lowers costs, to recovered prices, to high profit margins, and then to the sector bouncing back.

Slide 31 of Class 9 states the awkward result for both regions. The work aims at a green shift, and yet oil and gas still dominates, unintentionally.

A weaker answer would call reproductive agency simply bad. A stronger answer would say what it maintains, and where that shows up.

Remember: inhibition is a path kept in place, including when the speeches point the other way.

<!-- check: What does slide 31 of Class 9 say still dominates both regions? | Oil and gas, unintentionally, even though the work aims at a green shift. -->

## 7. The Norway example
<!-- readings: Fløysand et al. 2022, pp. 15, 19, 22; Bækkelund 2021, pp. 757, 765 -->

The job of this part is the case. Slide 40 of Class 9 asks for examples that you analyse, not for a place name dropped in at the end. Use the case to show both sides.

Hordaland, now part of Vestland, is described as organisationally thick and diversified. The institutional work there is collective and runs across clusters. Fløysand and colleagues say that work legitimised a green shift and supported clustering. That is the promote side, and it is still a claim about legitimacy, not a measured emissions cut.

Rogaland is organisationally thick and petroleum-specialised. The work there is individual, and it is tied to individual strategic leadership. The same authors say that work delegitimised a green shift while petroleum activity bounced back. That is the inhibit side.

The two regions matter because they are two petroleum regions that shared a green vision, and the work still diverged. Do not say one region had motivated people and the other did not. The finding is about different kinds of institutional work.

Bækkelund's villages are a different case. Flåm, Odda and Sogndal share institutions and landscape, and Flåm shows more change agency. Use them if you need a second illustration of change versus reproduction. Do not treat a tourism path as if it were the petroleum finding.

Remember: one pair of regions, two directions of institutional work, both sourced.

## 8. Discussion and conclusion
<!-- readings: Class 9, slides 41, 43; Fløysand et al. 2022, p. 15; Bækkelund 2021, p. 758 -->

The job of this part is the close. Slide 41 of Class 9 says to sum up what you discussed and to answer the question. If you made a claim in the introduction, you can return to it. Slide 43 adds that the conclusion should link back to theory and to the case, and that you should state a limit.

There are three points to land. First, agency promotes sustainable transformation when change work, shared across actors, legitimises a new path, as in the Hordaland account and in the battery market built by procurement. Second, agency inhibits it when reproductive work and specialised leadership keep petroleum at the centre, as in the Rogaland account, even where the vision is green. Third, intention is a weak test. The slide says the dominance of oil and gas was unintentional.

A limit belongs here. Bækkelund's range comes from rural tourism, so it does not by itself settle a petroleum result. The petroleum result is the Fløysand study. Say that, and you have shown a boundary instead of hiding it.

A weaker answer would end on a slogan about green growth. A stronger answer would answer the question in the words of the two sides, then name the limit.

Remember: answer the question, then name what the case does not show.

## 9. Traps and upgrades
<!-- readings: Hekkert et al. 2007, p. 421; Bækkelund 2021, p. 758; Fløysand et al. 2022, p. 15; Class 9, slide 43 -->

The job of this part is to retire the lines that sound clever and are not what the course says. Slide 43 rewards readings that are understood, not a smoother story than the readings give you.

The first trap is the model answer's line that a green technique does not change a system by itself. Instead, use the correction. Hekkert and colleagues, 2007, say a system needs entrepreneurs. They do not use that green-option sentence.

The second trap is to treat reproductive agency as simply a block on sustainability. Instead, say what Bækkelund says. It can be resistance, and it is also action in its own right. That study does not judge it as unsustainable.

The third trap is to explain Hordaland and Rogaland by who wanted change. Instead, use the finding. They shared a green vision. The work legitimised the shift in one region and delegitimised it in the other.

The fourth trap is a definition of sustainable transformation taken from outside the course. Instead, use the exam-day line from Fløysand and colleagues, citing UNEP, or Schlaile's normative questions. Flag anything else as outside the readings.

The fifth trap is a memorised script. Instead, keep the five moves and write the sentences yourself.

Remember: prefer the corrected course line, and say so when a model answer goes past it.

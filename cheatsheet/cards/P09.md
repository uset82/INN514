# Paper 9: Hekkert et al. (2007)
<!-- card: P09 | cite: Hekkert et al. 2007 | pages: 413-432 | class: 6 | themes: TH6 -->
*Functions of innovation systems: a new approach for analysing technological change*, Technological Forecasting and Social Change 74(4).

## In one line
The paper replaces structures with seven activities, and shows how a few can set a system moving.

## Key concepts
- **Technological innovation system** (*teknologisk innovasjonssystem*): citing Carlsson and Stankiewicz, a network of agents that create, spread and use a technology under shared institutions. (p. 416)
- **Function** (*funksjon*): the activities that produce technological change, studied instead of comparing structures. (p. 415)
- **Guidance of the search** (*retning for søket*): the function that sets expectations and goals, and so steers where a technology goes. (p. 423)
- **Motors of change** (*endringsmotorer*): the few functions that start a virtuous cycle and pull the others along. (p. 426)
- **Vicious cycle** (*ond sirkel*): negative fulfilment in one function that slows the others and can stop progress. (p. 427)

## Model
**Three motors of change** (p. 426, Figure 2):
1. Motor A: lobbying for market formation sets off entrepreneurial activity and knowledge.
2. Motor B: lobbying for research resources raises expectations about the technology.
3. Motor C: guidance of the search sets goals, then resources and knowledge follow.

## Findings
- Fulfilling one function affects the others, so the model is non-linear and full of interactions. (p. 426)
- Developments often start from a few functions that pull the rest. (p. 426)
- First Dutch studies found the functions fitted the data well. (p. 428)

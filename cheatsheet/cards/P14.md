# Paper 14: Bækkelund (2021)
<!-- card: P14 | cite: Bækkelund 2021 | pages: 757-768 | class: 5 | themes: TH5 -->
*Change agency and reproductive agency in the course of industrial path evolution*, Regional Studies 55(4).

## In one line
Bækkelund follows three fjord villages and finds reproductive agency doing more than blocking change.

## Key concepts
- **Industry path** (*næringsbane*): citing Fredin and colleagues, an activity that gathers enough critical mass for self-reinforcing effects. (p. 758)
- **Agency** (*aktørskap*): citing Sotarauta and Suvinen, action or intervention by an actor to produce a particular effect. (p. 758)
- **Change agency** (*endringsaktørskap*): Schumpeterian entrepreneurship, institutional entrepreneurship and place-based leadership. (p. 759)
- **Reproductive agency** (*reproduktivt aktørskap*): the counterparts, replicative entrepreneurship, institutional work and maintenance leadership. (p. 759)
- **Critical juncture** (*kritisk veiskille*): a moment that can move a path into creation, if an opportunity space opens. (p. 765)

## Model
**A range from change to reproductive agency** (p. 760, Figure 1):
1. Change: Schumpeterian entrepreneurship.
2. Change: institutional entrepreneurship.
3. Change: place-based leadership.
4. Reproductive: replicative entrepreneurship.
5. Reproductive: institutional work.
6. Reproductive: maintenance leadership.

## Findings
- Reproductive agency is stronger before formation and in path development; change agency is stronger in path creation. (p. 765)
- A shift into path creation often follows a critical juncture with an opportunity space. (p. 765)
- Public actors can enact Schumpeterian entrepreneurship, as in Flåm. (p. 765)

## Norway link
- **Flåm, Odda and Sogndal**: three fjord villages with the same institutions and landscape but different tourism paths. (p. 761)
- **Flåm**: more change agency than Odda and Sogndal. (p. 765)

## Exam use
- Dec 2024 Q2: both kinds of agency decide whether a path turns or stays. (our reading)

## Limits
- The cases come only from rural tourism, an industry with peculiarities of its own. (p. 766)

## Hook
Two kinds of agency, one path's life.

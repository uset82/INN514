## 1. Read the question
<!-- readings: Class 9, slides 33–43 -->

In what way can a regional innovation system promote or inhibit industrial restructuring? Use examples.

The job of this part is to take that question apart before you write. Slides 34 and 35 of Class 9 ask you to show how you read the task.

There are three parts. First, the subject is a regional innovation system. Second, the command is promote or inhibit, so both sides are required, and the change in view is industrial restructuring. Third, use examples, so the cases must be Norwegian and you must be able to name their sources. Those were the three parts.

Only Question 1 is compulsory. You then choose Question 2 or Question 3. The two answers are weighted equally, and no aids are allowed. Answers can be tested for plagiarism, so learn the moves and use your own words.

A weaker answer would describe one hopeful cluster and stop. A stronger answer would keep both sides and tie them to the two regions.

One way to say it: By this question, I understand a demand for both sides and sourced Norwegian cases.

Remember: both sides, then the two regions, each with its source.

## 2. Your plan in one minute

The job of this part is to give you a plan you can say aloud. Slide 39 of Class 9 asks the introduction to show how you read the question and how you will answer it.

Here is the thesis. A regional system promotes restructuring when related variety and collective work open a new path, and it inhibits restructuring when a specialised system and individual work keep the old path in place.

## 3. The introduction
<!-- readings: Class 9, slides 39, 43; Fløysand et al. 2022, p. 16 -->

The job of this part is the introduction. Slide 39 of Class 9 says to present how you understand the question and how you have chosen to answer it. You may also name one main argument. Slide 43 then checks whether the problem statement follows the question you were given.

In plain words, the opening tells the reader the route. More precisely, you say that restructuring here means a change of industrial path, not a new product on its own. You will use the four trajectories, and you will test both sides on two petroleum regions that shared a green vision.

A regional innovation system, as Fløysand and colleagues cite Cooke, is interacting subsystems of knowledge infrastructure and commercial exploitation. You do not need the whole definition in the first lines. You do need the reader to see that the system can enable a new path and can also hold an old one.

A weaker answer would open with a history of oil in Norway. A stronger answer would state the route and the two regions in a few lines.

One way to say it: To discuss how a regional system can help or hold back restructuring, I will use the four trajectories, then the two regions.

Remember: the introduction states the route and the two regions.

## 4. The theory you need
<!-- readings: Fløysand et al. 2022, p. 16; Fløysand & Jakobsen 2016, pp. 328, 330; Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 431; Bækkelund 2021, p. 758 -->

The job of this part is the theory. Slide 40 of Class 9 says to start from the readings, clarify the concepts, and put those readings to use. Revision three explains the regional system in full. Here you only need the pieces this question uses.

In plain words, the system is how a region learns, and restructuring is a change in the path that learning supports. Fløysand and colleagues, citing Cooke, give the system as interacting knowledge and exploitation subsystems. Fløysand and Jakobsen, 2016, name four trajectories. Exhaustion, when an industry loses its base. Extension, more of the same. Renewal, upgrading the industry with new products. Creation, a new industry. The model answer swaps those names for extension, upgrading, diversification and creation. Prefer the four the reading names. Upgrading is its word for renewal, not a separate fourth path, and diversification is not one of the four.

Related variety is the promote mechanism. Industries that differ but are related give a region room to combine them. Fagerberg and colleagues, citing Arthur, add path dependency. History favours some solutions and holds others back. The 2016 reading links that dependency to lock-in when change is blocked.

Bækkelund, 2021, separates change agency, which alters a path, from reproductive agency, which keeps a path going. Reproductive agency is not only resistance, and the reading does not judge it as unsustainable.

One way to say it: By industrial restructuring, I understand a change of path, from extension of the old one toward renewal or creation.

Remember: four named trajectories, related variety on the promote side, and lock-in on the inhibit side.

## 5. How it can promote
<!-- readings: Fløysand & Jakobsen 2016, p. 330; Fløysand et al. 2022, p. 15; Njøs et al. 2020, pp. 279, 281, 282; Bækkelund 2021, p. 758 -->

The job of this part is the promote side. The question's command requires it, and slide 40 of Class 9 asks you to relate the readings to the case.

There are three ways, and each one stays inside a reading. First, related variety lets a region combine industries that differ but are connected, so renewal need not start from nothing. Fløysand and Jakobsen expect more cross-industry innovation where that variety is related. Second, collective institutional work can legitimise a green shift. That is the Hordaland account. The system is diversified, and the work runs across clusters. Third, a region can point an existing industry at a new use. Njøs and colleagues say maritime battery technology is embedded in southwestern Norway's maritime industry. A cluster set a direction, and public procurement created a market.

Change agency is the kind of work that fits this side. Bækkelund describes it as the agency that alters a path. Do not say the old skills were simply transferred into offshore wind. The battery reading says the technology is embedded. It does not show that transfer.

A weaker answer would say a diversified region is innovative. A stronger answer would name related variety, the work, and the reading.

Remember: promotion is related variety, collective work, and a new use that stays tied to the industry the region already has.

## 6. How it can inhibit
<!-- readings: Fløysand & Jakobsen 2016, p. 328; Fløysand et al. 2022, pp. 15, 22; Bækkelund 2021, p. 758; Class 9, slide 31; Njøs et al. 2020, p. 268 -->

The job of this part is the inhibit side. If you only write the hopeful half, you have not answered the question. Slide 43 of Class 9 looks for analysis that can be critical.

In plain words, the same kind of system can hold a region on the old path. More precisely, path extension is more of the same. Fløysand and Jakobsen link path dependency to lock-in when change is blocked. A specialised region is less able to change course. In Rogaland the system is petroleum-specialised, and the institutional work is individual, tied to individual strategic leadership. That work delegitimised a green shift and reproduced the petroleum path.

Reproductive agency keeps a path going. It can look like resistance, and it is also action in its own right. Do not call it, on Bækkelund's authority, a block that has been judged unsustainable. The reading does not say that.

Slide 31 of Class 9 states the result for both regions. The work aims at a green shift, and yet oil and gas still dominates, unintentionally. Everyday action can favour the old industry even when people endorse the new aim.

The model answer adds that path creation depends on actors outside the region. Njøs and colleagues list multiscalar dynamics as a topic. They do not state that dependence. Leave the stronger claim out.

Remember: inhibition is extension and lock-in, specialised individual work, and a green aim that the old path still outlasts.

## 7. The Norway example
<!-- readings: Fløysand et al. 2022, pp. 15, 22; Njøs et al. 2020, pp. 273, 279; Fløysand & Jakobsen 2016, p. 337 -->

The job of this part is the case. Slide 40 of Class 9 asks for examples that you analyse, not for a place name dropped in at the end. Use one pair of regions for both sides, and keep a second illustration clearly separate.

Hordaland is the promote side. It is organisationally thick and diversified. Collective work legitimised a green shift and supported new green activity. Firms, universities and support bodies being present is not what did that. The reading says the same vision produced opposite results, so presence alone does not guarantee restructuring.

Rogaland is the inhibit side. It is organisationally thick and petroleum-specialised. Individual strategic leadership kept the petroleum path. The work delegitimised the green shift while petroleum activity continued.

A new path, where you need one, is the battery case. Njøs and colleagues say it is explained by the link between regional assets and the traits of the technology, not by either side alone. The technology is embedded in the maritime industry. Say embedded. Do not say the subsea skill was moved into offshore wind. The reading does not show that.

Mongstad's microalgae pilot is a different illustration. It would join oil knowledge to marine knowledge in fish feed. Use it only as a picture of related variety, not as the finding about the two regions.

Remember: one pair of regions, two directions of work, and the battery path kept as embedded, not transferred.

## 8. Discussion and conclusion
<!-- readings: Class 9, slides 41, 43; Fløysand et al. 2022, p. 15; Fløysand & Jakobsen 2016, pp. 328, 330 -->

The job of this part is the close. Slide 41 of Class 9 says to sum up what you discussed and to answer the question. Slide 43 adds that the conclusion should link back to theory and to the case, and that you should state a limit.

There are three points to land. First, a regional system promotes industrial restructuring when related variety and collective work open renewal or creation, as in the Hordaland account and in the battery market built by procurement. Second, it inhibits restructuring when extension, lock-in and specialised individual work keep petroleum at the centre, as in the Rogaland account, even where the vision is green. Third, the organisations being there is a weak test. The same vision produced opposite legitimacy outcomes.

A limit belongs here. The 2016 reading says which driver matters most cannot be settled, and one of its interviews was not designed for this question. Say that, and you have shown a boundary instead of hiding it.

A weaker answer would end on a slogan about diversification. A stronger answer would answer the question in the words of the two sides, using the four trajectory names, then name the limit.

Remember: answer the question, then name what the comparison does not settle.

## 9. Traps and upgrades
<!-- readings: Fløysand & Jakobsen 2016, p. 328; Njøs et al. 2020, pp. 268, 276; Bækkelund 2021, p. 758; Class 9, slide 43 -->

The job of this part is to retire the lines that sound clever and are not what the course says. Slide 43 rewards readings that are understood.

The first trap is the model answer's trio, that restructuring modernises an old industry, moves into related work, or opens a new sector. Instead, use the four names. Exhaustion, extension, renewal and creation.

The second trap is a different set of four: extension, upgrading, diversification and creation. Instead, keep exhaustion in the list. Upgrading is the reading's word for renewal. Diversification is not one of the four.

The third trap is to say petroleum engineering was turned toward offshore wind. Instead, say what Njøs and colleagues say. Subsea skill belongs to the oil region, and battery technology is embedded in the maritime industry. The reading does not show that wind transfer.

The fourth trap is to treat reproductive agency as a judged block on a needed green change. Instead, say it keeps a path going, and that it is not only resistance. The reading does not call the effect unsustainable.

The fifth trap is a memorised script. Instead, keep the five moves and write the sentences yourself.

Remember: four names, in the reading's order, and no transfer the reading does not show.

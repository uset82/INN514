## 1. Read the question
<!-- readings: Class 9, slides 33–43 -->

How can technologies in innovation systems promote or inhibit sustainable transformation? Use examples.

The job of this part is to take that question apart before you write. Slides 34 and 35 of Class 9 ask you to show how you read the task.

There are three parts. First, the subject is technologies, set inside innovation systems. Second, the command is promote or inhibit, so both sides are required. Third, use examples, so the cases must be Norwegian and you must be able to name their sources. Those were the three parts.

Only Question 1 is compulsory. You then choose Question 2 or Question 3. The two answers are weighted equally, and no aids are allowed. Answers can be tested for plagiarism, so learn the moves and use your own words.

A weaker answer would praise a green technology and stop. A stronger answer would keep both sides and tie each side to a sourced Norwegian case.

One way to say it: By this question, I understand a demand for both sides and sourced Norwegian cases.

Remember: both sides, then cases you can source.

## 2. Your plan in one minute
<!-- readings: Class 9, slides 39–41; Hekkert et al. 2007, p. 416; Steen et al. 2024, p. 2 -->

The job of this part is to give you a plan you can say aloud. Slide 39 of Class 9 asks the introduction to show how you read the question and how you will answer it.

Here is the thesis. A technology promotes sustainable transformation when the system around it builds a direction and a market, and it inhibits that change when those activities stay weak or when the path only extends the old one.

There are five moves. First, say how you read the question, including both sides. Second, define a technological innovation system and its functions, including the extra one that capture needs. Third, show how a technology can promote the change. Fourth, show how it can inhibit that change. Fifth, use maritime batteries and Norway's capture projects, then close on the question. Those were the five moves.

A memory aid of our own: read it, name the system, both sides, then the two cases.

This is the minute to learn by heart. The last chapter will ask for it again. Do not memorise a full script. The marks come from the moves.

Remember: five moves, and the thesis is about the system around the technology.

## 3. The introduction
<!-- readings: Class 9, slides 39, 43; Fløysand et al. 2022, p. 17 -->

The job of this part is the introduction. Slide 39 of Class 9 says to present how you understand the question and how you have chosen to answer it. You may also name one main argument. Slide 43 then checks whether the problem statement follows the question you were given.

In plain words, the opening tells the reader the route. More precisely, you say that a technology does not change a system by sitting there. You will look at the activities around it, and you will test both sides on two Norwegian cases.

Sustainable transformation, as the exam-day sheet takes it from Fløysand and colleagues, citing UNEP, is change that cuts emissions and protects biodiversity. You do not need a longer definition in the first lines. You do need the reader to see that the question is about that kind of change.

A weaker answer would open with a list of green gadgets. A stronger answer would state the route and the two cases in a few lines.

One way to say it: To discuss how technologies can help or hold back this change, I will use the functions, then two Norwegian cases.

Remember: the introduction states the route and the cases.

## 4. The theory you need
<!-- readings: Hekkert et al. 2007, pp. 416, 421–425; Steen et al. 2024, pp. 2, 3; Schlaile et al. 2017, pp. 4, 5; Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 431 -->

The job of this part is the theory. Slide 40 of Class 9 says to start from the readings, clarify the concepts, and put those readings to use. Revision six explains the functions in full. Here you only need the pieces this question uses.

In plain words, a technological innovation system is the actors, networks and institutions around one technology. Hekkert and colleagues, 2007, citing Carlsson and Stankiewicz, describe it as a network of agents that create, spread and use a technology under shared institutions. They list seven functions, from entrepreneurial activity to the creation of legitimacy. One function affects the others, so the list is not a recipe you walk through in order.

Steen and colleagues, 2024, keep those seven and add an eighth, materialization, because capture needs large plant. They also show a way to judge a system. Map the actors, rate each function as strong, intermediate or weak, and aim policy at the weak ones.

Schlaile and colleagues, 2017, keep the aim honest. A conventional path need not serve a social vision, so a technology is not sustainable on its own. They take the term dedicated innovation system from Pyka. Fagerberg and colleagues, citing Arthur, add path dependency. History favours some solutions and holds others back.

One way to say it: By a technological innovation system, I understand the actors and rules around one technology, judged by what they do.

Remember: seven functions from Hekkert, eight once materialization is added, and a technology is not sustainable on its own.

## 5. How it can promote
<!-- readings: Njøs et al. 2020, pp. 276, 279, 281, 282; Steen et al. 2024, pp. 4, 9, 10; Hekkert et al. 2007, p. 423 -->

The job of this part is the promote side. The question's command requires it, and slide 40 of Class 9 asks you to relate the readings to the case.

There are three ways, and each one stays inside a reading. First, guidance of the search can set a direction. Hekkert and colleagues describe that function as the one that sets expectations and goals. Second, a cluster and public procurement can create a market. Njøs and colleagues report that for maritime batteries, and they report that some seventy ferry lines followed. The technology covers onboard systems and the charging that supplies ships, and it is now strongly embedded in southwestern Norway. Third, a large project can be the exception to a weak function. Steen and colleagues name Longship and Northern Light as the main exceptions to weak materialization. Longship is a full-scale project with capture sites, ship transport and offshore storage.

That is promotion only if you keep the source. Embedded does not mean the old skills were recombined. The readings do not say that. And a project being built is not the same as a strong system.

A weaker answer would say green technology helps. A stronger answer would name the function and the reading.

Remember: promotion is a direction, a market, or a real project where a weak function had an exception.

## 6. How it can inhibit
<!-- readings: Steen et al. 2024, pp. 9, 10; Njøs et al. 2020, p. 281; Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 431; Schlaile et al. 2017, p. 5 -->

The job of this part is the inhibit side. If you only write the hopeful half, you have not answered the question. Slide 43 of Class 9 looks for analysis that can be critical.

In plain words, the same technology can sit in a system that does not carry it. More precisely, Steen and colleagues rate market formation, resource mobilization and materialization as weak in the Norwegian capture system. Njøs and colleagues put that system in preformation. There are few dedicated firms, no market, and the policy is top down. Mongstad was an earlier plan for a full-scale plant at the refinery and power station. It is not the project now being built.

Table 3 does not rescue the hopeful reading. It rates knowledge, experimentation and legitimacy as intermediate, not as strengths. The model answer calls them strengths. Prefer the table.

A conventional path can also hold the old system in place. Schlaile and colleagues say it need not serve a social vision. Fagerberg and colleagues, citing Arthur, say history favours some solutions and holds others back. That is the inhibit side. It is not a list of habits and skills the model answer adds.

A weaker answer would call every weak function a moral failure. A stronger answer would name the rating and the phase.

Remember: inhibition is weak functions, a system still in preformation, and a path that history holds in place.

## 7. The Norway example
<!-- readings: Njøs et al. 2020, pp. 273, 279, 281; Steen et al. 2024, pp. 4, 9 -->

The job of this part is the case. Slide 40 of Class 9 asks for examples that you analyse, not for a place name dropped in at the end. Use two cases, one for each side, and keep them distinct.

Maritime batteries are the promote case. The path is not explained by the region alone or by the technology alone. Njøs and colleagues say the link between regional assets and the traits of the technology is what has to be studied. A cluster set the direction. Public procurement created the market. The technology is embedded in southwestern Norway. Say that, and stop. Do not add a life-cycle sum. No course reading assesses the materials, the manufacturing or the electricity behind the batteries.

Norway's capture history is the inhibit case, with one exception you must not hide. Sleipner dates from 1996 and Snøhvit from 2008. Longship adds capture sites, ship transport and offshore storage, and with Northern Light it is the exception to weak materialization. The wider system still has a weak market, weak resources and weak materialization, and Njøs and colleagues still place it in preformation.

An informant in the Steen study says acceptance rose because capture was not sold as a way to prolong fossil activity. Do not reverse that sentence.

Remember: batteries for the promote side, capture for the inhibit side, and Longship named as the exception.

## 8. Discussion and conclusion
<!-- readings: Class 9, slides 41, 43; Steen et al. 2024, p. 9; Njøs et al. 2020, p. 281; Schlaile et al. 2017, p. 5 -->

The job of this part is the close. Slide 41 of Class 9 says to sum up what you discussed and to answer the question. Slide 43 adds that the conclusion should link back to theory and to the case, and that you should state a limit.

There are three points to land. First, a technology promotes sustainable transformation when guidance, a market and real plant line up, as in the battery market built by procurement, and in Longship as an exception to weak materialization. Second, a technology inhibits that change when the functions around it stay weak and the system stays in preformation, as in the wider capture system, or when the path only extends what history already favours. Third, intention is a weak test. A dedicated system, the term taken from Pyka, is one that points the path on purpose.

A limit belongs here. Steen and colleagues note that other interviewees might have produced slightly different ratings, and that capture innovation is international, so a wider geographical lens would help. Say that, and you have shown a boundary.

A weaker answer would end on a slogan about green technology. A stronger answer would answer the question in the words of the two sides, then name the limit.

Remember: answer the question, then name what the ratings do not settle.

## 9. Traps and upgrades
<!-- readings: Hekkert et al. 2007, pp. 421, 426; Steen et al. 2024, pp. 2, 9; Fagerberg et al. 2009, p. 431; Njøs et al. 2020, p. 279; Class 9, slide 43 -->

The job of this part is to retire the lines that sound clever and are not what the course says. Slide 43 rewards readings that are understood.

The first trap is to treat Hekkert's seven functions and Steen's eight as the same list. Instead, say that Steen and colleagues add materialization because capture needs large plant.

The second trap is the model answer's line that a working technology fails to spread when markets, finance or rules are missing. Instead, use what the reading shows. Hekkert and colleagues show functions interacting. They do not state that failure case.

The third trap is to call knowledge, experimentation and legitimacy strengths of the capture system. Instead, use Table 3. Those three are intermediate. Market formation, resource mobilization and materialization are weak.

The fourth trap is to say battery skills were recombined, or to weigh the climate gain from materials and electricity. Instead, say the technology is embedded. The life-cycle claim is outside the course. So is the rebound effect. Flag both.

The fifth trap is a memorised script. Instead, keep the five moves and write the sentences yourself.

Remember: seven and eight are different lists, the table says intermediate, and two popular warnings are outside the course.

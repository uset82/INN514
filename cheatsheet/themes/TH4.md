## Core definitions
- **Entrepreneurial ecosystem** (*entreprenørielt økosystem*): citing Hawley, a system marked by co-evolution, mutualistic interdependence and diverse, nested actors, which begins when entrepreneurs' paths cross. (Stam & van de Ven 2021, p. 811)
- **Munificence** (*rikelighet*): how many resources an ecosystem holds, from finance to talent. (Spigel & Harrison 2018, p. 161)
- **Functionality** (*funksjonalitet*): how easily entrepreneurs can reach those resources, through networks and trust. (Spigel & Harrison 2018, p. 161)
- **Selection effects** (*seleksjonseffekter*): opportunity costs and social legitimacy decide who becomes an entrepreneur, before any venture starts, through opportunity costs. (Autio et al. 2014, p. 1100)
- **Strategic choice effects** (*strategiske valgeffekter*): what those who enter then judge desirable and feasible, once they have started. (Autio et al. 2014, p. 1100)

## Key models and figures
**Ten elements in three groups** (Stam & van de Ven 2021, p. 813):
1. Institutional arrangements: formal institutions, culture and networks that set the rules.
2. Resources: infrastructure, finance, leadership, talent, knowledge, services and demand.
3. Output: productive entrepreneurship, the firms that commercialise and grow.

**Four ecosystem types** (Spigel & Harrison 2018, p. 163):
1. Strong: many resources, well-functioning networks, as in Silicon Valley.
2. Arid: sparse resources, well-functioning networks, as in some developing economies.
3. Irrigated: many resources, poorly functioning networks.
4. Weak: sparse resources, poorly functioning networks, as in deindustrialised regions.

# Paper 4: Schlaile et al. (2017)

*Innovation systems for transformations towards sustainability? Taking the normative dimension seriously*, Sustainability 9(12).

## In one line
Innovation systems need a paradigm shift, the authors argue, before they can serve sustainability.

## Key concepts
- **Innovation system** (*innovasjonssystem*): citing Niosi and colleagues, multiple interacting agents whose work produces, develops, protects, finances or regulates science and technology. (p. 2)
- **Paradigm** (*paradigme*): a set of assumptions, concepts, values and practices that make up a shared worldview. (p. 4)
- **Dedicated innovation system** (*dedikert innovasjonssystem*): from Pyka, a system dedicated to transformations towards sustainability. (p. 4)
- **Transformative innovation** (*transformativ innovasjon*): citing Steward, novelty that breaks the paradigm and changes the whole system. (p. 5)
- **Transformational failures** (*transformasjonssvikt*): citing Weber and Rohracher, the failures that a sustainability policy must also address. (p. 5)

## Model
**Three normative questions** (pp. 5–7):
1. Directionality: what to transform or sustain.
2. Legitimacy: why, and who decides.
3. Responsibility: by and for whom.

## Findings
- Technological fixes cannot solve wicked problems; economy, institutions, technology, culture and organisation must all change. (p. 2)
- The existing strands are scattered; only a shift at paradigm level can integrate sustainability's normativity. (p. 10)
- A dedicated system can in principle put innovation trajectories under a sustainability goal. (p. 10)

## 1. The theme in one minute
<!-- readings: Bækkelund 2021, pp. 758, 759; Njøs et al. 2020, p. 268 -->

In plain words, agency is what actors do. A new path does not appear by itself, and it does not appear because a region has the right statistics. Someone has to act.

Bækkelund, 2021, takes a short definition from Sotarauta and Suvinen. Agency is action or intervention by an actor to produce a particular effect, whether intended or not. The last words matter. Work can produce an effect its author did not plan.

Njøs and colleagues, 2020, describe path creation as the rise of a new development trajectory in a region, such as a new industry. Put the two together and you have the theme. New paths come from agency, and agency comes in more than one kind.

The Norwegian case is a set of three villages. Flåm, Odda and Sogndal share institutions and the same surroundings, and their tourism paths developed differently. That is the design the theme uses, and it is a good one, because it holds the setting still and lets the action vary.

Remember: agency is action with an effect, and a new path is what that action can create.

## 2. The key ideas
<!-- readings: Bækkelund 2021, pp. 759, 765; Garud & Karnøe 2003, p. 278 -->

Four ideas carry the theme.

The first is the pair of agencies. Following Bækkelund, change agency alters a path, and reproductive agency keeps it going. Neither is good or bad in itself. A region that only changes loses what it built, and a region that only reproduces cannot move.

The second is that the pair covers more than entrepreneurship. Change agency includes Schumpeterian entrepreneurship, institutional entrepreneurship and place-based leadership. Reproductive agency includes their counterparts, replicative entrepreneurship, institutional work and maintenance leadership.

The third is that agency is distributed. Garud and Karnøe, 2003, studied wind power among Danish and American actors, and they found that no single hero carried the path. A new technology comes from actors in different domains, each with its own frame, and the frame decides what an actor even notices.

The fourth is bricolage. The same authors take the word from Levi-Strauss and use it for resourcefulness and improvisation with what is at hand, in modest and steady steps. The opposite is a breakthrough, an attempt at dramatic outcomes.

Remember: change and reproduction, three forms of each, distributed agency and bricolage.

## 3. The model, step by step
<!-- readings: Bækkelund 2021, p. 760; Miörner & Trippl 2017, p. 486 -->

There are two models, and both are lists you can say aloud.

The first is the range from change to reproductive agency. On the change side: Schumpeterian entrepreneurship, new combinations. Institutional entrepreneurship, rewriting the rules. Place-based leadership, rooted in the place. On the reproductive side: replicative entrepreneurship, copying what works. Institutional work, keeping the rules. Maintenance leadership, keeping the place steady.

The second model comes from Miörner and Trippl, 2017. They describe three modes of change. First, layering, which adds something new to the support structure. Second, adaptation, which changes what exists. Third, novel application, which reuses what exists in a new way.

The models work together. The first tells you what kind of agency is at work. The second tells you what an actor does to the environment around the path. If you can move between them in one paragraph, you can answer either question about agency.

Remember: a range with three forms on each side, and three modes of change.

## 4. Norway examples
<!-- readings: Bækkelund 2021, pp. 761, 765; Njøs et al. 2020, pp. 281, 282 -->

The three villages are the main example.

Flåm, Odda and Sogndal share institutions and surroundings, and their tourism paths differ. Bækkelund, 2021, reports a pattern across the three cases. Reproductive agency is stronger before a path forms and in path development, and change agency is stronger in path creation. Flåm shows more change agency than the other two villages.

There is a second pattern. A shift into path creation often follows a critical juncture, and a critical juncture needs an opportunity space. Without the space, the moment passes and the path stays where it was.

The batteries give you a second case. Njøs and colleagues, 2020, describe a cluster that set a direction and public procurement that created a market, and some seventy ferry lines followed. That is change agency in a regional system, and it is a useful contrast with the villages. It also shows that a new path needs a buyer as much as a founder. A technology can be ready, and it still waits until someone commits to purchase it.

Remember: three villages, change stronger when a path is created, and batteries as a case of direction plus procurement.

## 5. How the exam asks about it
<!-- readings: Bækkelund 2021, p. 765; Class 9, slide 38 -->

The discussion question of December 2024 asks how agency in innovation systems can promote or inhibit sustainable transformation.

Here is the shape of a good answer. Define agency, then separate change from reproduction. Name the three forms on each side, so the examiner hears that you know the range. Then choose a case. In the three villages, change work was stronger in path creation, and reproductive work kept the older paths alive. In the batteries, a cluster and a procurement decision moved a technology into a market.

Then answer both sides. Agency promotes transformation when change work finds a critical juncture and an opportunity space. It inhibits transformation when reproductive work keeps an old path comfortable and unquestioned.

One honest sentence at the end helps, and it costs you nothing. The same actor can do both kinds of work, and the same work can have both effects over time.

There is also a move that shows judgement. Say what the theme cannot settle. The three villages share a setting, and that makes the comparison clean, but it also means the findings belong to rural tourism. Do not stretch them to an industrial region without a word of caution.

Remember: define, separate, name the forms, use a case, and answer both sides of the question in your own words.

## 6. Test yourself
<!-- readings: Bækkelund 2021, pp. 759, 765; Garud & Karnøe 2003, p. 281 -->

Question one. What separates change agency from reproductive agency?

Pause the audio and answer in your head.

Here is the answer. Change agency alters a path, and reproductive agency keeps it going. Both are work, and both can be deliberate.

Question two. What did Garud and Karnøe find in the wind power case?

Pause the audio and answer in your head.

Here is the answer. Danish actors with modest resources became world leaders, against better funded American actors, because agency was distributed and the path was built in steady steps.

Question three. Why is a critical juncture not enough on its own?

Pause the audio and answer in your head.

Here is the answer. Because it also needs an opportunity space. A moment of crisis opens a door, and actors still need room to walk through it.

Remember: two kinds of agency, a range of forms, and a path that opens when a moment, a space and a buyer all meet.

# Paper 6: Spigel & Harrison (2018)
<!-- card: P06 | cite: Spigel & Harrison 2018 | pages: 151-168 | class: 4 | themes: TH4 -->
*Toward a process theory of entrepreneurial ecosystems*, Strategic Entrepreneurship Journal 12(1).

## In one line
The authors turn the entrepreneurial ecosystem from a policy slogan into a process through which entrepreneurs acquire resources.

## Key concepts
- **Entrepreneurial ecosystem** (*entreprenørielt økosystem*): better understood as an ongoing process, not a thing, through which entrepreneurs acquire resources, knowledge and support. (p. 158)
- **Chaotic conception** (*kaotisk begrep*): citing Sayer, a concept that describes rather than explains, which the ecosystem idea had become, and the authors want to give it a theory. (p. 152)
- **Munificence** (*rikelighet*): how many resources an ecosystem holds. (p. 161)
- **Functionality** (*funksjonalitet*): how easily entrepreneurs can reach those resources. (p. 161)
- **Recycling** (*resirkulering*): founders who return as investors and mentors, and workers who carry skills to other ventures. (p. 160)

## Model
**Four ecosystem types** (p. 163, Figure 2):
1. Strong: many resources, well-functioning networks.
2. Arid: sparse resources, well-functioning networks.
3. Irrigated: many resources, poorly functioning networks.
4. Weak: sparse resources, poorly functioning networks.

## Findings
- Access to resources matters more than their presence. (p. 159)
- Recycling depends on a culture that does not punish failure. (p. 160)
- Strong, well-functioning ecosystems should see more growth-oriented entrepreneurship. (p. 163)

## Norway link
- No Norwegian case in this paper.

## Exam use
- Practice: an ecosystem can promote transformation by giving access, or inhibit it by also holding those resources back. (our reading)

## Limits
- The propositions are offered to guide research, not tested here. (p. 165)
- Defining ecosystems by their activity levels would be circular. (p. 164)

## Hook
A process, not a place.

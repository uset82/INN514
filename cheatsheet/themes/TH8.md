## Core definitions
- **Crowdfunding** (*folkefinansiering*): collective financing that also works as an instrument to foster innovation, and lets users act like venture capitalists. (Bargoni et al. 2024, p. 1110)
- **Incremental innovativeness** (*inkrementell innovativitet*): citing Chan and Parhankangas, a cumulative improvement of existing technology and capabilities, which builds on what a firm already does. (Bargoni et al. 2024, p. 1115)
- **Radical innovativeness** (*radikal innovativitet*): citing Chan and Parhankangas, change that is more drastic, unique and comprehensive, and opens something genuinely new. (Bargoni et al. 2024, p. 1115)
- **Value proposition** (*verdiforslag*): citing Frow and colleagues, a mechanism that adjusts as actors negotiate how resources are shared, so the offer changes as the relationship changes. (Quero & Ventura 2019, p. 52)
- **Crowdfunding ecosystem** (*folkefinansieringsøkosystem*): a service ecosystem in which actors cocreate value to carry one actor's proposal forward, which makes backers, platforms and institutions one system. (Quero & Ventura 2019, p. 48)

## Key models and figures
**Five steps of value cocreation**, adapting Frow and Payne (Quero & Ventura 2019, p. 51):
1. Identify actors.
2. Determine core values.
3. Facilitate dialogue between the actors.
4. Identify cocreation opportunities.
5. Cocreate value propositions with the actors.

**Six dimensions of each platform** (Demattos Guimarães et al. 2024, p. 8):
1. Country.
2. Geographical scope.
3. Business model.
4. Focus of the platform.
5. Institutional structure.
6. Foundation year.

- **Intro:** I read the question as asking how agency can promote or inhibit sustainable transformation, and I answer with crowdfunding communities, one ecosystem, one platform case and a conclusion.
- **Theory:** Bargoni et al. (2024) treat crowdfunding as more than money: it finances innovation at lower cost and tests projects outside the organisation. Quero and Ventura (2019) add that its actors cocreate value propositions, so backers are not only customers but partners in the offer.
- **Example:** Quero and Ventura (2019) show that two Spanish platform directors added public institutions to the actors of the ecosystem. The same case shows that crowdfunding can be read as agency, because backers decide together which projects go ahead, and that decision is the agency.
- **Mini-conclusion:** Crowdfunding communities promote transformation when they move resources to new projects, and inhibit it when only a few platforms set the terms and pick the winners before any campaign starts.

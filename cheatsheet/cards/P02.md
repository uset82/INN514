*The “trinity” of innovation: actors, ideas and objects in innovation practice*, Conference paper, 5th Nordic Geographers' Meeting, Reykjavík (paper in preparation, do not quote).

## In one line
The paper asks how actors, ideas and objects meet in practice, and answers with a typology.

## Key concepts
- **Trinity of innovation** (*treenighet*): citing Schwarz and Thompson, patterns of people, things and ideas behind technology. (PDF p. 9)
- **Actant** (*aktant*): citing Latour, any entity that acts or is treated as acting, nature included. (PDF p. 9)
- **Necessary relations** (*nødvendige relasjoner*): citing Sayer, the abstract properties of innovation that hold in any setting. (PDF p. 3)
- **Contingent relations** (*kontingente relasjoner*): cultural economy, history, governance and territory, which give the necessary relations content. (PDF p. 12)
- **Pragmatic innovation complex** (*pragmatisk innovasjonskompleks*): firms as actors, tools as the objects, and tasks as the ideas. (PDF p. 10)

## Model
**The pragmatic innovation complex** (PDF p. 10, Figure 1):
1. Company and tools meet through technique.
2. Tools and tasks meet through production, with unintended outputs.
3. Company and tasks meet through rationale, profit.

## Findings
- Innovation is discursive, material and practical, and the material side must not be lost. (PDF p. 14)
- Profit and productivity govern innovation in capitalist economies, but other interests count. (PDF p. 12)
- Public authorities and environmental groups can push innovation towards emission cuts. (PDF p. 13)

## Limits
- The authors do not judge whether innovations work well, only how. (PDF p. 13)
- Necessary relations alone cannot capture interaction, so qualitative study is needed. (PDF p. 13)

# Paper 1: Fagerberg, Mowery & Verspagen (2009)
<!-- card: P01 | cite: Fagerberg et al. 2009 | pages: 431-444 | class: 1 | themes: TH1, TH3 -->
*The evolution of Norway's national innovation system*, Science and Public Policy 36(6).

## In one line
Norway grew fast with little research; the authors explain why through system, history and structure.

## Key concepts
- **National innovation system** (*nasjonalt innovasjonssystem*): all learning and innovation across an economy, not only universities and laboratories; the authors trace it to Freeman, Lundvall and Nelson. (p. 431)
- **Institutions** (*institusjoner*): citing North, the "rules of the game", costly to establish but scale-giving once adopted. (p. 433)
- **Path dependency** (*stiavhengighet*): historical processes that favour some solutions and hold others back, say the authors citing Arthur. (p. 431)
- **Absorptive capacity** (*absorpsjonskapasitet*): citing Cohen and Levinthal, the ability to identify, absorb and use new knowledge. (p. 437)
- **Dutch disease** (*hollandsk syke*): currency appreciation after a resource discovery, which can bring de-industrialization. (p. 434)

## Model
**Three industrial development paths** (p. 439):
1. Small-scale decentralized path: small firms investing little of their own funds.
2. Large-scale centralized path: metals, electrochemistry and hydropower, often foreign-financed.
3. Knowledge-intensive network-based path: R&D-intensive firms in sectors such as ICT.

## Findings
- Adjusted for industrial structure, Norwegian firms do as much business research as peers abroad in five of six comparisons. (p. 436)
- The authors name knowledge diffusion and cooperation in innovation as the system's strong point, which standard indicators miss. (p. 438)
- The modernizers' computer research failed commercially, but its competences paid off in oil and gas. (p. 440)

## Norway link
- Domestic suppliers in shipbuilding, engineering and services grew with petroleum, instead of Dutch disease. (p. 434)

## Exam use
- Dec 2024 Q1: take the broad definition and institutions as rules of the game. (our reading)

## Limits
- The authors leave aside the social and cultural roots of the system's adaptability. (p. 442)

## Hook
Broad system, slow history, low research, high income.
